# M1 — Elevator Pitch

## What this block decides

The shortest true statement of what the product is and what it does for the buyer — the version that survives being repeated by someone who does not work at the company.

`M1` comes before `M2` deliberately. A pitch that cannot be written in three sentences is evidence that `P2` is not decided. Writing it first surfaces that while it is still cheap to fix.

## What this block is not

- Not a tagline. Taglines are memorable; pitches are informative. A tagline may come out of `M4`.
- Not a description of features.
- Not a promise larger than `P2` allows. The pitch cannot write the product.

## Required sections

1. **Category.** What the buyer files this as. Either an existing category the buyer already budgets for, or a new one — and if new, `M2` must be told, because category creation is a multi-year education spend.
2. **The pitch.** Three sentences maximum: what it is, who it is for, what changes. In the buyer's vocabulary from `P4`, not the company's.
3. **Why this framing.** Which alternative framings were rejected and what each would have cost. A pitch without rejected alternatives was not chosen.
4. **The claim being made.** Stated plainly, separated from the phrasing. The claim is what must be true; the phrasing is how it is delivered.
5. **Variants by persona.** One version per persona in `P4`, each leading with what that persona is measured on. Same claim, different entry point. If two variants make different claims, the pitch is not settled.
6. **Variant by context.** Written form, spoken form, and the one-line form for a subject line or a bio. Length changes what survives.
7. **Proof attached.** The single strongest piece of evidence available today, from `P2` section 8, that can be delivered in the same breath. A pitch with no attachable proof is a claim the listener has to take on faith.
8. **Forbidden claims.** What the pitch must never say, because it is unproven, staged for later per `P2`, or legally constrained. This list travels to `V4` and `V5`.
9. **The misunderstanding test.** The most likely wrong conclusion a listener draws from the pitch. Every compression creates one. Name it and adjust or accept it deliberately.

## Evidence rules by regime

- **Greenfield.** The category choice is the highest-stakes decision here. Default to an existing category unless there is a specific reason the product cannot be evaluated inside one.
- **Extension.** State how the pitch relates to the parent product's pitch. Two products from one company that pitch incompatibly cost both of them.
- **Repositioning.** Write today's pitch and the new one side by side, and state what a current customer hearing the new one would conclude about what they bought.

## Quality tests

- Read it to someone outside the company. Can they repeat the claim, not the words, ten minutes later?
- Could a competitor use the same three sentences? If yes, it is a category description.
- Does the pitch survive removal of every adjective?
- Does it name who it is for, specifically enough to exclude someone?

## Canonical extract

```
M1 — Elevator Pitch
Category:            <what the buyer files it as> ; existing or new: <which>
Pitch:               "<three sentences>"
Core claim:          <the claim, separate from the phrasing>
Persona variants:    <one line each, by persona>
Attachable proof:    <the strongest single item>
Forbidden claims:    <what must never be said>
Likely misreading:   <the wrong conclusion this framing invites>
```

## Probing questions

- What does the listener think you are when you finish the first sentence?
- Which existing budget line would a CFO put this under?
- What is the shortest version that still excludes the wrong buyer?

## Contradictions to watch

- The pitch promises something absent from `P2` → the pitch is writing the product.
- The category chosen here is not the category `M2` will position in → the market hears two and remembers neither.
- The pitch requires the listener to already know the `P1` problem, while `P3` says most of the ICP is unaware → the pitch works only in the wrong room.
