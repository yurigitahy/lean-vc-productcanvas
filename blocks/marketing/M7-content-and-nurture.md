# M7 — Content and Nurture

## What this block decides

What the company publishes and sends, to move a buyer from their current belief to purchase readiness — organized by the awareness ladder in `P3`, not by content format.

## What this block is not

- Not an editorial calendar. Dates come later; this block decides the argument.
- Not SEO planning, though keywords may follow from it.
- Not a list of formats. Format is the last decision, chosen after audience, stage, and argument.

## Required sections

1. **Narrative spine.** The argument the company makes across all content, in five to seven beats, from the buyer's current belief to the conclusion that this product is the right response. This is the outline of the whole content operation; individual pieces are instances of a beat.
2. **The conceptual enemy in practice.** How the `M2` enemy shows up as a concrete, recognizable situation the buyer will admit to. Abstract enemies produce abstract content.
3. **Content by awareness stage.** For each stage in `P3` section 8 — unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, ready — what the buyer believes, what question they are asking, what content answers it, and what the next action is. Content built without stage discipline talks to product-aware buyers and wonders why the top of the funnel is empty.
4. **Content inventory.** The specific pieces, grouped by beat and stage. For each: audience persona, the belief it targets, format, and effort. Rank by ratio of belief-change to effort rather than by enthusiasm.
5. **Anchor assets.** The three to five substantial pieces everything else references: the diagnostic, the benchmark, the framework, the calculator, the teardown. Anchor assets do the work; the rest distributes them.
6. **Proprietary angle.** What the company can publish that no competitor can, because of its data, position, or experience. Content that anyone in the category could write competes on volume, which is the worst axis available.
7. **Nurture tracks.** For each entry point in `M5`, the sequence of messages, what triggers advancement, and what triggers exit. Include the track for people who are right but not now — most of the ICP at any moment.
8. **Cadence and volume.** What is realistically sustainable with the resources named. An unsustainable calendar produces three good months and then silence, which costs more than a modest calendar sustained.
9. **Voice application.** How the `M4` rules apply to each format, with the traps specific to each: long-form drifts formal, social drifts to superlatives, email drifts to urgency.
10. **Sales reuse.** Which pieces sales will use directly in `V5`, and what has to be true about their format for that to work. Content built with no sales use is half an asset.
11. **Retention use.** Which pieces serve existing customers in `C6`. Reuse across acquisition and retention is the main lever available to a small content operation.
12. **Measurement.** What each stage's content is expected to move, and how that is observed. Traffic is not a belief change; name the observable proxy.

## Evidence rules by regime

- **Greenfield.** Content carries the entire education load before there is a sales team. Weight the inventory toward the earliest stages.
- **Extension.** Existing audience and existing content are assets. State what is reused, what is retired, and what would confuse the parent product's audience.
- **Repositioning.** Existing content encodes the old position and continues to rank and circulate. Name what must be rewritten, redirected, or removed. This is usually the largest single task in a repositioning.

## Quality tests

- Does the narrative spine reach a conclusion, or does it stop at problem awareness?
- Could a competitor publish the anchor assets? If yes, section 6 has not been answered.
- Is there a track for buyers who are right but not now?
- Is the cadence achievable with the team that exists, this quarter?

## Probing questions

- What can you publish that would make a competitor uncomfortable because it is true?
- What does the buyer read the week before they start looking for a solution?
- Which piece would a salesperson send after a first call, and does it exist?
- What happens to a lead who is qualified and not buying for nine months?

## Contradictions to watch

- Content sounds like the company's existing content rather than the `M4` voice → the voice was defined and ignored.
- The inventory targets product-aware buyers while `P3` says most of the ICP is problem-unaware → the content talks to a stage the market is not in.
- Cadence assumes resources not present in `P8`.
