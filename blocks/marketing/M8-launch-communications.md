# M8 — Launch Communications

## What this block decides

The story that makes the market pay attention when the product becomes available, where that story runs, and what the launch is expected to produce.

A launch is a moment of concentrated attention, not a status change. This block decides whether that moment is used or spent.

## What this block is not

- Not the launch project plan. Sequencing of commercial actions is `V8`; the two must agree on dates.
- Not a press release. It may produce one.
- Not the content plan. `M7` is continuous; this is a spike.

## Required sections

1. **The launch story.** Why this is newsworthy to someone who does not work at the company. Products are not news; changes in what is possible are. If no such change can be named, say so and plan a commercial launch without a press motion rather than a press motion that fails.
2. **The angle by audience.** Trade press, general business press, the ICP's own community, existing customers, partners, and internal teams each need a different entry to the same story.
3. **Proof for the story.** Data, cases, benchmarks, or first customers, from `P2` section 8 and `P7`. A launch story with no proof is an announcement, which travels a fraction as far.
4. **Channel plan.** Which channels from `M5` carry the launch, in which order, and why. Include the owned channels first — a launch that starts on rented channels is a launch that starts by paying for its own audience.
5. **Sequence.** The order of moments: internal enablement, existing customers, partners, community, press, paid amplification. Getting this wrong — customers learning from press — costs more than it saves.
6. **Timing.** The date, why that date, and what it collides with: category events, competitor cycles, buyer budget calendars, holidays in the target geography.
7. **Influencers and voices.** Who already speaks to this ICP with credibility, what would make them care, and what is being asked of them. Distinguish those with the ICP's attention from those with volume.
8. **Assets required.** What must exist by when: landing page, demo, one-pager, case study, video, pricing page, launch email. Each with an owner. Cross-check against `M7` — most should already be planned there.
9. **Launch metrics.** What the launch is expected to produce: reach, qualified conversations, pipeline, sign-ups, independent coverage. With numbers, and with the statement of what would count as failure.
10. **Post-launch continuity.** What runs in the four weeks after, so the spike hands off to the `M7` cadence instead of ending. Most launches lose more to silence in week three than to a weak week one.
11. **Risks.** What could go wrong publicly — a competitor announcement, an outage during the spike, an unflattering question the position invites per `M2` section 9 — and the prepared response for each.

## Evidence rules by regime

- **Greenfield.** There is no existing audience to launch to. Weight the plan toward borrowed credibility: partners, communities, and voices from section 7.
- **Extension.** The existing base is the strongest launch channel and should hear it first. State the message to existing customers who did not buy this.
- **Repositioning.** The launch has to explain a change, which means acknowledging what came before. Decide whether to name the shift explicitly or let the new position simply replace the old, and say which and why.

## Quality tests

- Would a journalist covering this category find section 1 newsworthy without the company's help?
- Do existing customers hear about this before the press?
- Does every asset in section 8 have an owner and a date?
- Does the plan state what failure looks like, in numbers?
- Do the dates here match `V8` exactly?

## Probing questions

- What is the one sentence a journalist would use as a headline, and is it true?
- Who finds out from a stranger instead of from you, and how do they feel about that?
- What is running the Monday after launch week?
- What does a competitor say the day you announce?

## Contradictions to watch

- Launch uses channels not mapped in `M5` → either the map is incomplete or the launch is improvising.
- Dates disagree with `V8` → two teams, two calendars, one launch.
- The story relies on proof `P2` staged for later.
