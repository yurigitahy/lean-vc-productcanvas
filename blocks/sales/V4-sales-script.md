# V4 — Sales Script

## What this block decides

The structure of the conversation: what happens in what order, what must be established before advancing, and what the seller is trying to accomplish at each point. A script is a structure with prepared language at the hard moments, not a text to be read.

## What this block is not

- Not a monologue. If the seller is talking more than the buyer in the first half, the script is a presentation.
- Not a demo outline alone.
- Not a substitute for `V3`. This block sequences what `V3` produced.

## Required sections

1. **Call map.** The conversation stages with a time budget for each and the outcome that defines completion. The seller should know at any moment which stage they are in and what still has to happen.
2. **Opening.** How the call starts: context, why now, and the agenda proposed in one breath. Prepared language, because openings are where inexperienced sellers lose control of the frame.
3. **Qualification sequence.** The `V1` criteria that require discovery, asked in an order that feels like a conversation rather than a form. Includes the disqualification path — how to end a call early, respectfully, when the fit is not there. Sellers who cannot end calls carry dead pipeline.
4. **Discovery.** The `V3` ladder in sequence, with the branch points: what to ask next depending on the answer. Two or three branches, not a decision tree.
5. **The turn.** Where the conversation moves from the buyer's situation to the product. Triggered by an observable condition — the buyer has named the conflict from `V3` section 3 — not by elapsed time. Turning early is the most common failure in a first call.
6. **Positioning statement.** The `M1` pitch delivered in context, adjusted to what the buyer just said. Prepared language, with the persona variants.
7. **Demo narrative.** If there is a demo: the sequence, which is a story about the buyer's situation and not a tour of the product. What is shown first, what is deliberately not shown, and how long. Demos that show everything convert worse than demos that show one thing convincingly.
8. **Proof delivery.** When to introduce evidence, which piece for which persona, and how to hand it over. Early proof answers a question nobody asked.
9. **Price conversation.** When it happens, how it is framed against the economic value in `P2`, and what is said when the buyer asks earlier than planned. Deflecting the price question badly costs more than answering it early.
10. **Objection handling in flow.** The three most likely objections from `V3` and how they are handled without derailing the conversation. Full treatment is `V3`; here it is the in-conversation move.
11. **Next-step ladder.** The asks by commitment level, and the rule for choosing: always the largest step the conversation has earned. A call ending with "I'll send some information" ended without a next step.
12. **Post-call.** What is sent, when, and what it must contain — including the summary of what the buyer said, in their words, which is the single highest-leverage follow-up habit available.

## Evidence rules by regime

- **Greenfield.** The script is a first draft. Instrument it: which questions produce useful answers, where calls stall, which openings get agenda agreement.
- **Extension.** State what is inherited from the existing script and what must be different, especially the turn and the price conversation.
- **Repositioning.** Sellers have muscle memory for the old pitch. Name explicitly what must stop being said.

## Quality tests

- Does the buyer talk more than the seller in the first half?
- Is there a defined path to ending a call early?
- Is the turn triggered by an observable condition rather than by a clock?
- Does the script contain any claim on the `M1` forbidden list?
- Does every call end with an ask larger than "think about it"?

## Probing questions

- At what minute does your best seller start talking about the product?
- How does a seller end a call with a lead who does not fit?
- What does the follow-up email say that the buyer would forward internally?
- Which part of the demo do buyers stop watching?

## Contradictions to watch

- The script pitches before qualifying against `V1` → the most expensive resource spent on the least likely accounts.
- The demo narrative shows staged capability from `P2` as if available today.
- The next-step ladder assumes a commitment level the sale type in `V2` does not support at that stage.
