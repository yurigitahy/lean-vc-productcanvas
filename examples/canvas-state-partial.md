## Canonical extracts

### P1 — Problem and Opportunity
```
Core problem:        The contractor carries the risk of documents held by parties with no incentive to maintain them.
Top three symptoms:  Retained payments; coordinator time chasing; crews refused at gates.
Status quo:          Shared spreadsheet, one owner, updated from memory.
Cost of inaction:    ~0.4 FTE plus retained payments; tail risk of a refused claim (unevidenced).
Who feels it:        Compliance coordinator ; who can fix it: Operations director / CFO
Urgency triggers:    Insurance renewal; client audit; halted work at a gate.
Strongest pain:      Inability to prove compliance on demand.
Evidence base:       14 interviews, 8 months tickets, 3 lost deals. No subcontractor evidence.
```

### P2 — Value Proposition
```
What it is:          Software that collects, verifies, and monitors subcontractor compliance documents.
Core promise:        Compliance provable for any crew on any date, without assembling it.
Mechanism:           Documents collected from the subcontractor directly, verified on receipt, monitored to expiry.
Economic value:      Coordinator time plus retained-payment cycle; tail risk unpriced pending P1 validation task.
Replaces:            A spreadsheet and part of a coordinator's role. No incumbent vendor in 12 of 14 accounts.
Main substitutes:    Two insurance-certificate point solutions (no licences); manual chasing; acceptance.
Differentiation:     Subcontractor-side collection, not contractor-side storage; durable ~2 years.
Not in scope:        Site access control; payment release; subcontractor sourcing.
Staged:              Automated verification of licence authenticity — month 9, not at launch.
```

### M1 — Elevator Pitch
```
Category:            Compliance management — existing category, buyer already budgets for it.
Pitch:               "Facilities contractors are accountable for crews they do not employ. We collect and monitor subcontractor compliance documents at the source, so any crew on any site can be proven compliant on the day it worked."
Core claim:          Compliance can be proven on demand without assembling it.
Attachable proof:    Existing product's certification data on 40 accounts.
Forbidden claims:    Anything implying licence authenticity verification (staged, month 9).
Likely misreading:   That this replaces site access control.
```

### M2 — Market Positioning
```
Strategy:            Competition — against point solutions and the spreadsheet.
Compared against:    Spreadsheet first, insurance-certificate tools second, in-house build third.
Winning axis:        Coverage of all document types, collected at the source.
Conceded:            Insurance-certificate tools have deeper broker integrations.
Conceptual enemy:    The belief that holding a document is the same as being covered.
Punchlines:          Three, each with proof obligation; one currently unmet — the refused-claim frequency.
Education needed:    Low. Buyer already has the category and the budget line.
Objections created:  "We already have a tool for certificates"; "our subcontractors will not use a portal".
```
